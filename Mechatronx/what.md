## Disc rot keep-in-checker?
### Will need:
- Moisture detector
- Temperature detector
- Light sensor (maybe)
- Some way to detect ultraviolet rays? (REALLY maybe)
- Multicoloured LEDs and/or a speaker.
### This relates by:
I am a PC software archivist. Many of my older PC games/programs are on CD-ROMs or DVD-ROMs. I am worried about some rotting. Some have already rotted. (rest in pepperonis Eureka's 601 Games Volume 2, and Spongebob RotFD for PS2 (in some part) even though they werent my fault.) Many, more discs I just own, and dont have as much incentive to archive, but I would still like to keep the discs' longevity.
#### Really specific cases where I don't think it would be worth it making things for them because the discs are either really common or are really not necessary to archive or i just dont collect them
- UK made PDO CDs. (Bronzing caused by acid.)
- LaserDiscs. (Don't collect them)
- Warner HD DVDs (Don't collect them)
- Flexplay (They already died. Besides, they most likely have stock US DVD ISOs on them.)
- Warner Bros-distributed DVDs from 2005 to 2009 (Most likely only US DVDs affected.)
### Progress guide:
1. Find a way to make each individual sensor work on its own.
  A. Moisture detector should detect humidity. Should not be over ~45%. I don't want moisture to slip into the CD.
  B. Temperature detector should detect extreme heat or extreme cold. Extreme heat causes warping and potential cracking. Extreme freeze is more only for CD-Rs, but delamination on any CD or DVD can happen with fluctuations between extreme heat and cold. It can also condense moisture in discs.
  C. (maybe) Light sensor / that way to detect UV rays are to prevent direct sunlight from shining on the discs. May cause a chemical breakdown / deterioration.
2. Find a way to make each sensor work in tandem.
3. Make an alert system to warn of what's wrong. (eg. 1 beep for too hot/cold, 2 beeps for too humid, 3 beeps for too bright.) A series of multicoloured LEDs might also help.
